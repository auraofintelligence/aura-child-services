FROM THE ORIGINAL PROPOSAL

Adults relationships and personal control

The learning relationship depends on adults who listen, respond and remain accountable. A useful personal record has little effect if nobody follows through on the young person's question or helps arrange the next opportunity. Care providers, carers, adoptive families, educators and mentors each retain the responsibilities relevant to their role.

A supported choice

The young person receives an understandable explanation of an activity, the information involved and available alternatives. They have a practical way to pause, correct something or leave. A request for help reaches a named adult or an independent route where appropriate. Ordinary care, schooling and family arrangements do not depend on completing the programme.

Legal authority for decisions follows the actual law and care arrangements. The young person's own understanding and decision-making capacity are considered for the particular decision. OAIC guidance does not set one universal age for making a privacy decision. Agreement to learning, recording, model training, research and publication concerns different purposes. [S10]

Public private and secret selves

Aura uses public, private and secret views to organise information and expression. A young person may choose to share an artwork while keeping the personal experience behind it private. A sensitive account receives the agreed protection. The service explains actual access, key holders, legal limits and recovery rather than promising secrecy that its arrangement does not provide. [P1]

A change of carer, adoption arrangement or device prompts a review of access and continuity. Official care records keep their statutory purpose and retention rules. The personal Aura holds chosen work and meaning under its stated arrangement. A correction to the working account remains traceable, with any required changes to official records handled through their own process.

Care in the actual setting

The host applies relevant child-safe requirements, worker screening, supervision, privacy and reporting duties. If harm is disclosed, an adult responds through the real safeguarding process. The AI does not investigate a child or decide whether a concern is credible. Appropriate clinical and therapeutic care has its own qualified practitioners. [S11-S12]

eSafety identifies risks in AI companions, including harmful material and dependency. The selected system therefore presents itself honestly as software, avoids exclusive or secret relationship demands and supports contact with real people. The review examines what actually happens when a child asks for help or a conversation becomes inappropriate. [S13]

The practical benefit of control is confidence in participation. A young person understands what is happening, knows which choices are theirs and has someone accountable to turn to when the arrangement falls short.
