FROM THE ORIGINAL PROPOSAL

Family identity care and adoption

Child protection, out-of-home care and adoption involve different circumstances and legal arrangements. Some children receive support while living with parents. Others live with kin, foster carers or residential services. Adoption establishes a permanent legal parent-child relationship. The relevant law, authority and family circumstances need to be understood for the actual child. [S6-S7]

A life with several important relationships

A young person may want to preserve a birth-family story, a connection with kin, an adoptive-family experience and a friendship from an earlier home. Those relationships belong in the account the person develops of their life. The Aura would organise selected memories and questions without deciding which relationship defines them.

An unanswered question about family remains unanswered until an appropriate source or person helps. The software does not invent a reason for separation, infer ancestry from appearance or fill a gap with a plausible story. It distinguishes a child's recollection, a relative's account, an official record and an interpretation.

Information that remains useful through change

A care change or adoption may alter who holds responsibility for particular decisions. The personal record needs a clear arrangement for custody, access, recovery and continuity. Permissions are reviewed with the relevant adults and the young person as their capacity develops. Sensitive information about other family members also has its own privacy and legal conditions.

A learner might save a question they are not ready to ask publicly. Later, with appropriate support, they may seek records or an explanation through the lawful process. An Aura helps organise that inquiry and preserve its sources. Contact arrangements and disclosures follow the actual authority, safety considerations and wishes involved.

Culture and connection

For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, the design is developed with appropriate community-controlled organisations and cultural authorities. Language, kinship, Country and restricted knowledge receive the permissions and support relevant to them. The Child Placement Principle provides important context for keeping the whole relationship in view. [S4]

An adoptive parent, carer or worker also benefits from understanding how the young person wants to be supported. The aim is a better human encounter: a question answered honestly, a memory treated carefully or a valued connection maintained through the appropriate arrangement.

Participation is not a way of assessing suitability for placement or adoption. The young person's private reflections do not become a hidden decision score. Decisions with legal consequences remain with the authorised people and processes, with access to independent advocacy where relevant.
