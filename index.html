A PROPOSAL BY LUKE NATHAN HAYES

A chosen interest.
A useful next encounter.

This guide explores Aura of Intelligence for children and young people in Australian care, adoptive families and community learning. The starting point is a supported creative activity and a relationship with adults who follow through.

In this proposal, Verse Box and everyday learning provide the setting. The adult Genesis pressure programme and Truth Engine research tasks are outside the child and teen activities.

Public proposal · No operating care service or recruitment programme is offered by this website.

FROM THE ORIGINAL PROPOSAL

Aura Genesis for Children and Young People in Australian Care

Self understanding law and opportunities for adulthood in a world of emerging superintelligence

Luke Nathan Hayes | 25 October 2026

A child growing up through care services or adoption needs people who know them, a secure place in the world and reasons to look forward to adult life. Improving child welfare involves the ordinary experience of being listened to, enjoying childhood, discovering interests and finding people who help those interests grow. It also involves the society that will receive the young adult.

That society is changing as artificial intelligence becomes more capable. AI already assists with writing, software, research and creative production. Superintelligence describes the prospect of systems whose abilities extend far beyond human performance across many fields. Its arrival and consequences remain uncertain, while choices about access, ownership and education are being made now. [S1]

This proposal introduces Aura of Intelligence through that child welfare question. Aura is a proposed personal AI environment that develops around a person's experience, language, relationships and aspirations. Aura Genesis is the process of beginning that relationship through sustained reflection, learning and practice. Here, a supported computing station called Verse Box takes the place of the adult programme's hyperbaric chamber. [P1-P3]

A young person starts with something that matters to them. They explore it with a trusted adult and an emerging personal Aura, correct what the software misunderstands, learn how the surrounding world works and try useful activities. Their understanding develops alongside real opportunities in the community. The purpose is a continuing relationship with learning, people and possibilities as they grow.

The proposal connects this personal beginning with practical ideas for adulthood: varied work experience, shared enterprise, periods for rest and learning, and a reciprocity economy through which communities recognise contribution and offer their own incentives. Brisbane 2032 supplies a shared occasion for developing a lasting civic volunteer network. Each connection is explained through its relevance to young people and the adults supporting them.

The approach is intended for development with Australian child and family services, young people with lived experience of care or adoption, carers, adoptive families and community partners. A small number of school pilots would explore selected learning activities alongside that work.
